The object

A rank-one locate-then-edit insert writes a key–value pair into one MLP. Merging several such updates by plain addition produces collateral interference: one fact moves another.

A closed-form key-cosine discriminant tracks that damage until perturbation gain and merge size push the system into a second regime. The geometry-valid g ≤ 5 window is the reference cell only; other cells can qualify at larger g. Do not read g ≤ 5 as a map-wide rule. Damage can still be gradated near g = 10; the law is not read as valid out to g = 10.

What is measured

  • Per-pair logit drop at merge sizes g ∈ {2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100}
  • Partial Spearman ρ(I_cos, drop | I_mag)
  • Perturbation gain = median |drop| / relative dose; cut at 8
  • Constructive fraction: share of drops < 0

Protocol

  • Phase 1 installs edits and dumps keys, values, and residuals.
  • Phase 2 merges random groups and records collateral drops.
  • Analysis rebuilds the operating curve on CPU from the shipped arrays.

Measurements

High gain (≥ 8)

Interference is mostly destructive. Geometry remains informative at small g (2, 3, 5). The geometry-valid g ≤ 5 window is the reference cell only; other cells can qualify at larger g.

Low gain (< 8)

Deeper layers. Constructive merges become common (14B: 81–88% of drops negative). Geometry still tracks the size of the cross-term; it does not flip into an anti-predictor. Gain is a rank-level screen, never a carrier.
