A Gain-Screened Two-Regime Law of Edit Federation Zeyu Fu a,∗ a State Key Laboratory of Trauma and Chemical Poisoning, Institute of Combined Injury, Chongqing Engineering Research Center for Nanomedicine, College of Preventive Medicine, Army Medical University, Chongqing, China Abstract Methods for combining independently computed weight updates—task arith- metic, adapter fusion, editing composition—share one assumption: overlap between updates is monotonically harmful, so alignment should be mini- mized or whitened away. We falsify it for rank-one knowledge edits. Across 22 model–layer cells spanning seven architecture families from 1B to 20B parameters, aligned cross-talk between merged edits reverses sign. In some layers merged edits damage one another as predicted; in others—deep layers of several architectures, at every scale tested—the same interference system- atically raises the member edits’ target logits before crossing to damage at an architecture-dependent group size. This is neither sign cancellation nor a failure of geometry to track cross-talk; what flips is that cross-talk’s effect at the readout. One measurable scalar, the layer’s perturbation gain, orders the regimes (rank correlation -0.82 with constructive fraction, p = 2.6 × 10 −6 , 22 cells; gate constants and the directional prediction frozen before the runs), and a first-order readout model grounds it: gain ranks cells by the magnitude of the readout slope at the edit’s operating point (a rank-level proxy, not a point estimate); the regime is that slope’s sign. Introduction Composing independently computed weight-space updates into a single network has become a standard operation on trained models: task vectors are added, adapters are fused, and low-rank corrections are stacked, all without retraining. The operation is a superposition of perturbations in one shared parameter matrix, and its central open question is a question about neural computation rather than about bookkeeping: when several perturbations are applied at once, how does the network’s readout combine them, and can the combined effect be anticipated from the perturbations themselves? Rank-one knowledge editing gives that question an unusually clean in- stance. Deployed models carry factual errors and stale or hallucinated asso- ciations that surface only after release [1], and correcting them one fact at a time is far cheaper than retraining. Operators therefore increasingly apply post-hoc knowledge edits: rank-one or low-rank weight updates that install, correct, or delete an individual fact in place [2, 3, 4]. Each such edit is a single rank-one perturbation of one linear map, specified by an explicit key/value pair, so the perturbation that one edit delivers at another edit’s key has a closed form in the stored vectors—no training probes, no fitted surrogate. As the practice matures, the unit of maintenance also shifts from a single edit to a fleet of them: several teams author updates independently—new product facts, corrected entities, retracted claims—and whoever owns the model must decide which to combine into the next build. We call this edit federation: merging many independently authored edits into one deployed model.
